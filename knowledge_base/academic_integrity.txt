================================================================================
ACADEMIC INTEGRITY POLICY
================================================================================

1. PLAGIARISM AND CHEATING DEFINITION
Plagiarism is presenting someone else's work, ideas, or language as your own without proper acknowledgement. This includes copying text directly from sources, paraphrasing closely without citation, purchasing papers, or using AI text generators to complete writing assignments without explicit instructor permission. Cheating includes copying another student's exam answers, using unauthorized materials during tests, or communicating with others during exams.

2. PENALTIES AND DISCIPLINARY ACTIONS
Violations of academic integrity, including plagiarism and cheating, are subject to serious penalties:
- First Offense: Typically results in a grade of zero (0) on the specific assignment or exam, and a formal report is filed with the academic dean.
- Second Offense: Results in an automatic failing grade (F) for the course and disciplinary probation.
- Repeat Offenses: Can lead to suspension or permanent expulsion from the university.

3. COLLABORATION POLICY
Collaboration is only allowed when explicitly authorized by the instructor in the syllabus or assignment prompt. If authorized, all student collaborators must be credited. Sharing code, solutions, or essays without authorization is considered a form of academic misconduct (unauthorized collusion).

4. WRITING SUPPORT AND CITATIONS
Students are encouraged to use correct citation formats (such as APA, MLA, or Chicago style) for all referenced material. The University Writing Center provides free, one-on-one tutoring and guidance on citations, writing structure, and research methodology to help students avoid accidental plagiarism.
